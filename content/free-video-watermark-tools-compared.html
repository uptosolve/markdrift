Search for a free video watermark tool and most results are video editors with a free plan. Free usually comes with a catch: the editor's own logo on your export, a length or resolution cap, an account, or an upload to someone else's server. We make one of these tools, MarkDrift, so read this with that in mind. What the table says

If "free" has to mean a clean export with nothing added, the full editors drop out first. Kapwing's free plan says outright that exports carry a watermark, and VEED's help centre says the same for free accounts and for people who aren't logged in. VEED also notes that its watermark shows up in a different part of the video after a while, which is the same idea as a moving watermark, used on you. Watermarkly's pricing page says its free version adds a Watermarkly logo next to yours. That wording is about photos, and the video page doesn't say otherwise, so check an export before you rely on it.

Canva is a special case. Its help centre says watermarks appear on premium elements when you're on the free plan. The page only mentions premium elements, so your own video with your own logo on it shouldn't get a Canva mark, though it's worth checking one export. It is still an upload to Canva, and none of the pages we read describe batching separate video files.

The browser-based tools, VidShift, Bannerbear's free tool and MarkDrift, all process video on your device, need no account and add nothing of their own. The difference between them is the watermark itself. VidShift offers nine fixed spots or a tiled grid. Bannerbear offers five fixed spots and a choice of showing the logo for the whole video or the first few seconds. MarkDrift adds moving, jumping and combined styles.

Recapo is the one hosted tool here with a real moving watermark, a diagonal bounce you can speed up or slow down. It runs in Recapo's cloud, needs an account, and uses credits after a free quota.

Shutter Encoder is the desktop pick. It's free with no limits, overlays an image with adjustable opacity, size and position, and has a render queue for many files. The Windows download is about 158 MB, and nothing leaves your computer.

Where MarkDrift is weaker

These are the places MarkDrift falls short of the tools above:

  • It only adds watermarks. There's no trimming, cropping, captions or editing of any kind. Kapwing, Canva and VEED are full editors, and VidShift links to separate trim and compress tools.
  • Speed depends on your device. A 3-minute 1080p clip took about 19 seconds in Chrome on a mid-range laptop in our test, but an old laptop or a phone will be slower. Cloud tools encode on their own servers, so your device matters less there.
  • A batch uses one setting for every video. Watermarkly lets you open "Preview & Adjust" and move or resize the mark on one file only. MarkDrift can't do that yet.
  • It's text or a logo, not both in one mark. Watermarkly can combine text and a logo in one design.
  • Saving a batch to a folder only works in Chrome and Edge. In Safari and Firefox each video downloads separately.

Which one to use

For one clip that also needs trimming or captions, a full editor makes sense, as long as you accept its limits. Kapwing's free plan stamps its own watermark, and Canva is clean for your own logo but needs an upload. For a batch where some files need their own tweaks, Watermarkly's per-file adjust helps, with the extra logo on the free version. For a big folder of files on a Windows or Mac computer, Shutter Encoder is free and keeps everything on your computer. For a batch of Reels or TikToks with a mark that moves or covers the frame, with no upload and nothing added, that's the job we built MarkDrift's batch tool for. Questions about free watermark tools

Which free video watermark tool doesn't add its own watermark?

Among the tools we checked, VidShift and Bannerbear's free tool say they add no watermark of their own, Shutter Encoder says it is free without limitations, and MarkDrift adds none. Canva adds watermarks only to premium elements on the free plan. Kapwing and VEED add theirs on free exports, and Watermarkly's pricing page says its free version adds a logo.

Kapwing or Canva for watermarking a video?

On the free plans, Kapwing adds its own watermark and caps exports at 4 minutes and 720p. Canva's help centre only mentions watermarks on premium elements for free users, and Canva accepts video uploads up to 1 GB. Both need your video uploaded to their servers.

Are these checks up to date?

They were made on 1 October 2026 from each tool's own pages. Free plans change often, so check the linked pages before you decide.

Why is so much marked "not stated"?

We only filled a cell when the tool's own page said it. Many pages don't mention batch processing or motion options at all, and we didn't want to guess.
