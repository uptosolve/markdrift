MarkDrift: the watermark tool

MarkDrift puts your name or logo on videos and photos, one file or a whole batch, right in your browser. We built it for creators and small shops who post a lot of short video and want their name on every clip. The tools we looked at first either charged credits, added their own watermark, or made you upload your files. This one does none of that. Your files stay on your device, and the result carries your mark and no mark of ours.

Each page below opens the same tool, set up for a different job, with its own how-to underneath.

  • Add a watermark to a video: the main tool. Text or logo, five styles, MP4 out.
  • Moving watermark maker: a mark that glides, bounces or jumps around the frame, so a crop can't remove it.
  • Watermark many videos at once: one setting for a whole folder of Reels or TikToks, vertical and landscape mixed.
  • Watermark photos: tiled or corner marks on JPG, PNG and WebP, with batches returned as a ZIP.
  • Add a logo to a video: your PNG logo in a corner, turned white if you like.

What every page has in common

Nothing is uploaded. The video engine runs on your own device through your browser's built-in video features, so your clips never reach a server. That also means speed depends on your machine. In our test, a 3-minute 1080p video took about 19 seconds in Chrome on a mid-range laptop, and phones are slower.

It works in Chrome and Edge 94+, Safari 17+ and desktop Firefox 130+. Older browsers can still do photos. Videos go in as MP4, MOV, WebM or MKV and come out as H.264 MP4.

It's free and open source under the MIT licence. You can read the code, or run your own copy, at github.com/uptosolve/markdrift.

No watermark can be made impossible to remove, and we don't claim otherwise. What MarkDrift does is make removal slower and messier than cropping a corner, which is where most reposting starts.
